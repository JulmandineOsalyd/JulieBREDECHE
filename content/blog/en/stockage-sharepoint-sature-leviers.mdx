With almost every client, I see the same issue. SharePoint storage quota is dangerously close to its limit.

⚠️ **Once the storage quota is exceeded, the tenant switches to read only mode. No more creating or editing, only viewing and deleting.**

Three combined mechanisms usually explain the storage explosion:

- **File versioning**: every edit creates a new version. A PowerPoint file can quickly reach 20 GB.
- **Large files**: training videos and high resolution scans weigh far more than standard office documents.
- **Inactive sites**: project finished, site forgotten, but still there accumulating content.

The first instinct is to buy more space. But the problem will come back fast if no real fix is put in place.

---

## How is SharePoint storage calculated?

Before acting, a benchmark. SharePoint storage runs on a shared pool, not site by site.

The official formula: **1 TB base per organization, plus 10 GB per eligible license purchased** (Business, Enterprise, etc.). A tenant with 100 licensed users therefore has 1 TB + 1 TB (100 x 10 GB), so **2 TB in total**.

![SharePoint storage quota calculation in the Microsoft 365 admin center](/images/blog/stockage-sharepoint-storage-quota.png)

This storage pool is shared across all SharePoint sites in the tenant. Users' OneDrive quotas are individual, separate from this SharePoint pool: 1 TB on average depending on the license.

**Three quick levers let you take back control of your SharePoint storage.**

---

## 1. Switch to manual storage quota management

In automatic mode, a single site can absorb a large share of the pool with no safeguard. <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/manage-site-collection-storage-limits" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Switching to Manual mode</a> in the SharePoint admin center changes the game.

![Storage management mode setting in the SharePoint admin center](/images/blog/stockage-sharepoint-storage-management.png)

New sites then get a default quota set by the organization, not a theoretical 25 TB ceiling. **25 to 100 GB is usually enough for a standard collaborative site**, to adjust for heavier document or media libraries.

The switch happens in two steps.

- **Set the standard for new sites**: an initial quota suited to everyday usage.
- **Review existing sites**: export the list of sites and their consumption, then apply consistent caps.

⚠️ This lever does not reduce storage already consumed, it caps future growth. Once in manual mode, it becomes an ongoing governance topic: monitoring alerts, handling extension requests.

---

## 2. Limit versioning and clean up version history

This is very often the lever that recovers the most storage.

### Set versioning upstream

Many SharePoint libraries keep 500 versions of a document by default. Reducing that number to 50 or 100 versions already covers most rollback needs.

![Setting the number of versions kept in a SharePoint library](/images/blog/stockage-sharepoint-versionning.png)

Microsoft also offers an interesting alternative: <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/plan-version-storage#determine-default-version-limits-for-new-document-libraries-in-your-organization" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">automatic version limits</a>. Instead of a fixed number, the algorithm trims the least useful intermediate versions itself, while keeping a complete recent history. A real topic, which I will cover in more detail in an upcoming article.

### Clean up the history already accumulated

Changing this setting does not delete versions already stored. A retroactive cleanup is needed: a PowerShell script that scans libraries, finds and deletes versions beyond a certain threshold.

⚠️ **The step that worries users the most. Deleting versions means deleting history.**

Three precautions before running anything.

- **Communicate before, never after.** Warn the teams involved that older versions will disappear, and why.
- **Keep a record of what gets deleted.** A detailed log (file, number of versions deleted, date) answers any question raised later.
- **Test on a pilot site.** Run the cleanup on a single site before rolling it out tenant wide.

---

## 3. Archive or delete inactive and oversized sites

An inactive site is not necessarily a useless site: closed project folders, contractual archives... It is a candidate for review, not a site to delete by default.

In most tenants I audit, a small number of sites hold most of the storage consumed. Three signals to build the review list:

- **Storage consumed:** prioritize the largest sites, which give a fast and effective win.
- **No recent activity:** 6 to 12 months without activity, a starting point to adjust based on the business.
- **Orphaned sites:** a site with no owner is a strong signal: no one will ever confirm whether to keep it, or its retention obligations.

The next step is to keep, archive, or delete these sites, based on the owner's answer and the applicable retention rules.

---

## Going further: thinking about storage as an ongoing effort

The levers above clean up what already exists and buy some time. It becomes essential to **put real governance in place** so you don't get overwhelmed again.

### Automate retention for inactive sites

Retention and lifecycle rules on SharePoint sites let you act continuously, without repeated manual work. They rely on site age, lack of activity, or the absence of an active owner.

<a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/inactive-site-policy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">SharePoint Advanced Management (SAM)</a> can automate the process of detecting inactive sites. But it comes with an added cost, and requires Microsoft 365 Archive to already be in place.

### Set up an archiving solution

Having an archiving solution removes the reluctance to delete: content is not lost, only moved to cheaper storage, and can be reactivated if needed.

<a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/archive/archive-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Microsoft 365 Archive</a> offers cold storage 75% cheaper than active storage. This extra cost only applies once the tenant's total storage exceeds the quota already included. Metadata, permissions, and versions are preserved during archiving.

Other solutions, internal or third party, can fill the same role. What matters is having an archiving tool in place before launching a large scale cleanup policy.

### Think about the lifecycle of workspaces from day one

A workspace should have a lifespan planned from the moment it is created: predefined workspace types, a clear naming policy, a mandatory review date. This kind of governance assumes, upstream, automated provisioning of SharePoint sites and Teams teams.

⚠️ These levers require upfront governance work, driven together with IT and business teams. Not quick wins, an investment that saves you from writing this same article again next year.

---

## My takeaway

Buying more storage without understanding what consumes it just delays the problem. It doesn't solve it.

These levers reflect what I see most often with my clients, not an exhaustive list. You could go further still: raising awareness among staff about data retention under GDPR, or routing certain file types, media, technical exports, to dedicated storage solutions.
